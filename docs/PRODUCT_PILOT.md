# Crunch Week: student value and a paid pilot

The core promise: turn checked assessment deadlines and genuinely free hours into a realistic next task. When work, training or an assignment changes, show what no longer fits and let the student approve a revised plan.

## Essential student journey

1. Enter one assessment manually or upload module documents. Review dates against the source; unknown dates stay outside the schedule.
2. Confirm study hours and protect lectures, work and training. Default hours are clearly labelled until confirmed.
3. Open Today and see the next task with its date, time and duration, or the issue preventing a realistic plan.
4. Log actual study, update remaining work, and review proposed changes when circumstances change. A completed study session is not a submission.
5. Export to a calendar and download a backup. Saved semesters currently live in the browser on that device.

## Test whether it earns payment

Run a two-week pilot with 5–10 students using their real module documents and schedules. Include students with work or training commitments. This is a proposed experiment, not evidence of demand.

- Observe whether each student reaches a checked, usable first plan without assistance; record setup time and where they get stuck.
- Check extracted deadlines against original documents. Record missed or incorrect assessments separately from corrections students make.
- Track return usage with consent: students returning across both weeks, logging sessions, and replanning around real commitments. Ask which task the app made easier.
- At the end, make a clear paid continuation offer and record actual acceptance. Compliments and hypothetical willingness to pay do not establish demand.
- Talk to university student-support staff about whether clash visibility solves a workflow they own, and what evidence, procurement and privacy requirements a pilot would need.

## Before charging for a hosted service

The current app is a local planner, not a multi-user service. It needs accounts with isolated data, reliable cloud saving and recovery, a suitable privacy/deletion process, and a per-account cost limit before offering a hosted student subscription. Its current chat quota is per installation, not per signed-in student. Calendar export is a download, not automatic two-way sync.

Prioritise a reliable setup-to-daily-use experience and evidence of repeat use before adding subscriptions, dashboards for institutions or further AI features. Do not promise improved grades, perfect extraction or deadline guarantees without evidence.
